## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Agrarian Violence & Patih's Cooperation (17 Apr 1825):** Tensions erupted in direct attacks by displaced Javanese villagers on colonial government-administered lands. Resident MacGillavry leaned heavily on Surakarta Grand Vizier Radèn Adipati Sasradiningrat II to identify, arrest, and extradite participants.
- **European Planters & British Capitalists (25 Mar 1825):** The request of Thomas Davison of London to join prominent British planter William Stavers in Surakarta demonstrates continued British commercial interest in Vorstenlanden estate agriculture despite Dutch regulatory restrictions.
- **North vs. South Coast Salt Economies (1 May 1825):** MacGillavry provides an extraordinary breakdown of inter-regional trade in Java. In the Eastern Mancanegara (Rawa/Tulungagung, Kalangbret, Ponorogo, Trenggalek, Pacitan), geographical mountain barriers made transport of government-monopolized North Coast marine salt prohibitively expensive. Consequently, local populations harvested unrefined salt along the rugged Indian Ocean surf (Kretek, Brosot, south of Bantul).
- **Yogyakarta–Kedu Economic Interdependence:** Yogyakarta exported southern sea salt to Kedu via the Progo valley markets (Samen, Kemloko, Magelang) in exchange for Kedu highland rice. MacGillavry noted that inland toll-gates (*bandar*) doubled salt prices, forcing impoverished peasants to consume minimal quantities.

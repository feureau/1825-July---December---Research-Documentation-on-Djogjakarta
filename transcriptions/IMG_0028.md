## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Rural Disorder & Bandits (April 1825):** Escalating rural criminality along the Yogyakarta–Surakarta border (Klaten) reflected worsening social distress. The involvement of young armed retainers connected with princely retinues (Pakualaman, Natakusuman) and roving underclass figures (*Jangki* / itinerant vagrants) who inhabited rural food stalls (*warung*) and market barriers (*begaar*) underscored the unraveling of rural policing.
- **Inter-Regional Religious and Political Infiltration:** Discontent in western Java (Cirebon, site of previous major anti-colonial uprisings in 1802–1806 and 1818) was spilling eastward into the borderland residency of Banyumas, highlighting cross-regional communication among anti-colonial dissidents months before Diponegoro's open call to arms.
- **Cholera and Colonial Therapeutics:** The persistence of the cholera epidemic led Patih Danureja IV to request commercial quantities of spirits (brandy) from colonial warehouses for distribution to rural village heads, illustrating both the severity of the mortality rate and the lack of scientific medical infrastructure.

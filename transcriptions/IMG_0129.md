## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0129.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Research Notes (Typewritten extracts from H.F. Aukes, *Het Legioen van Mangkoe-Negoro*, Bandung: A.C. Nix & Co., 1935, pp. 63–66)
- **Date Range:** 1825-07-20 to 1827-12-27
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Critical historiographical and military extracts from H.F. Aukes' *Het Legioen van Mangkoe-Negoro* (1935, pp. 63–66) analyzing the assault on Tegalrejo on 20 July 1825 and detailing the military structure, troop strength, and technical deficiencies of the Netherlands Indies Army (*KNIL*) and auxiliary forces at the outbreak of the Java War. Resident Anthonië Hendrik Smissaert and Patih Danureja IV (*DR*) send Pangéran Mangkubumi (*MB*) to Tegalrejo to demand Pangéran Dipanagara (*DP*) account for disturbing the peace; Dipanagara refuses out of fear of imprisonment, and when Smissaert threatens Mangkubumi with personal retribution, Mangkubumi defects and remains at Tegalrejo. On 20 July 1825, Smissaert orders a mixed military expedition—comprising troops from Paku Alam I, the child Sultan, and other princes—under First Lieutenant Thierry (7th Hussars; who died on 27 July 1825 in Yogya) with 25 flankers, 25 hussars, and two field guns, accompanied by Assistant Resident P.F.H. Chevallier. Halting within cannon range of Tegalrejo, Chevallier demands the 1,500 armed defenders disperse peacefully; met with rifle fire and slingstones, the colonial force opens fire with artillery and executes a cavalry charge, dispersing the defenders after 25 are killed. One colonial sergeant-major is killed and two hussars wounded; Dipanagara and Mangkubumi escape, and Tegalrejo is burned to the ground. Aukes emphasizes the catastrophic toll of the five-year war: 27 million Dutch guilders, 8,000 European fatalities, 7,000 native auxiliary casualties, and untold losses on Dipanagara's side. The extract also transcribes vital military statistics: in 1825, colonial forces numbered 639 officers, 6,148 Europeans, and 5,734 Javanese in 10 battalions; the Mangkunagaran Legion fielded 800 men (later expanding to 1,500); British commercial agents formed volunteer corps in Semarang and Surabaya (7 British merchant officers and 2 Armenians were killed at Dempeh/Demak on 3 September 1825); Daendels' *Jayeng-Sekars* (mounted police of elite Javanese youth) served the Residents; native auxiliary contingents were sent from Madura, Sumenep, Buton (700 men under Major Raja Haji Suleiman serving in Bagelen), Tidore (285 Alfurs), and Ternate (552 men); monthly metropolitan replacements of 300 troops; the arrival of the Netherlands Expeditionary Force on 27 December 1827; and severe technical shortcomings of infantry flint-lock muskets (rendered useless in tropical rains) and tight-bore rifles requiring hammer-driven ball loading.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Anthonië Hendrik Smissaert, Radèn Adipati Danureja IV (*DR*), Pangéran Mangkubumi (*MB*), Pangéran Dipanagara (*DP*), Paku Alam I, First Lieutenant Thierry, P.F.H. Chevallier, Major Raja Haji Suleiman of Buton, H.F. Aukes, Herman Willem Daendels.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta (*Jogja*), Tegalrejo (*TR*), Dempeh (Demak), Semarang, Surabaya, Bagelen, Buton, Tidore, Ternate, Madura, Sumenep, Netherlands.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Het Legioen van Mangkoe-Negoro* (1935), Assault on Tegalrejo (20 July 1825), Total Java War Cost (f. 27,000,000 / 15,000+ allied casualties), Netherlands Indies Army Strength (1825: 639 officers, 11,882 soldiers), Mangkunagaran Legion (800–1,500 men), British Volunteer Corps (Dempeh disaster), Jayeng-Sekars (*Djajeng-Sekars*), Outer Island Auxiliaries (Buton, Tidore, Ternate), Flint-Lock Rifle Technical Limitations.

## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Hydrological and Economic Divide of Java:** MacGillavry delineates the contrasting geography of the Mancanagara. Western Mancanagara (Banyumas) drains southward into the Indian Ocean via the Serayu River, rendering it isolated from northern trade routes. Eastern Mancanagara (Madiun, Kediri, Ponorogo) connects via the Brantas and Bengawan Solo rivers to the Java Sea.
- **Vision for Cilacap as a Deep-Water Port:** MacGillavry makes an early colonial proposal to open **Cilacap** as the primary southern deep-water harbor to connect Banyumas and the southern Principalities directly to maritime commerce.
- **Warning of Rural Revolt:** The Resident presciently warns Batavia against suppressing indigenous artisanal salt-making along the infertile southern coast (Bantul, Kulon Progo, Kebumen). Banning local salt would provoke explosive peasant bitterness and bankrupt essential toll-gates (*Bantul, Brosot, Kandangan, Kali Wedi*).
- **Criticism of Internal Tariffs:** MacGillavry concludes with an outspoken critique of colonial fiscal policy, urging Batavia to eliminate oppressive internal transit tolls and pasar fees that throttled commerce and impoverished the Javanese peasantry.

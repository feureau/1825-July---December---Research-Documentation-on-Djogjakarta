# Transcription of IMG_0119.jpg

## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0119.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI *Jogja Letters* No. 54)
- **Date Range:** 1826-05-27 to 1826-06-06
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Official dispatches of acting Resident Jan Izaäk van Sevenhoven (*Van S*) to High Government officials in Batavia between 27 May and 6 June 1826 preserved in ANRI *Jogja Letters* No. 54, chronicling severe socio-economic breakdown, famine, and monetary disruption. On 27 May 1826, Van Sevenhoven writes to H.J. van de Graaff and the Financial Council (*Financieele Raad*) rejecting a proposed municipal gambling tax on games of dice (*dobbelen*), explaining that: (1) the Chinese community is already ruined and deeply impoverished by the rebellion, (2) the administration lacks an effective police force to oversee gambling houses, and (3) societal consensus warns that legitimizing gambling would accelerate complete moral and social breakdown. On 1 June 1826, Van Sevenhoven reports to Commissioner-General Du Bus de Gisignies that Pajang (*Padjang*) has been cleared of insurgent forces and Rembang Ardom relieved after two days of intense combat, urging General De Kock to launch his general campaign swiftly to break remaining rebel resistance. However, these military movements provoke an acute humanitarian crisis as displaced populations flood back into Yogyakarta; with no financial means to pay returnees or native privates in colonial service, acute famine breaks out, causing daily starvation deaths (*"people dying of hunger daily"*). Local confidence in colonial currency collapses, resulting in a 50% devaluation of the silver guilder against copper *duits* and catastrophic inflation for market provisions. In pacification maneuvers, Tumenggung Kertadirja (*Tum Kerta Dirdja*) is seconded from Yogyakarta to Alang-Alang to reconcile the local peasantry; multiple bankruptcies among licensed Chinese opium tax-farmers are reported; and on 6 June 1826, Van Sevenhoven records the death of official government Javanese translator Johannes Godlieb Dietrée, who had lost his sight and hearing following the 1825 campaigns and was subsequently pensioned.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Jan Izaäk van Sevenhoven (*Van S*), H.J. van de Graaff, Leonard Pierre Joseph burggraaf du Bus de Gisignies, Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock, Radèn Tumenggung Kertadirja (*Tum Kerta Dirdja*), Johannes Godlieb Dietrée.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta (*Jogja / Djogja*), Pajang (*Padjang*), Rembang Ardom, Alang-Alang, Priangan, Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Gambling Tax (*games of dice*), Impoverishment of Chinese Community, Lack of Police Force, Humanitarian Refugee Crisis (*daily starvation deaths*), Monetary Collapse (*50% devaluation, guilders vs duits*), Opium Tax-Farmers (*bankruptcy*), Government Translator (*Dietrée disability and pension*).

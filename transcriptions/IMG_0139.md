- **Date Range:** `1825-07-25` to `1825-08-02`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Excerpts from the private correspondence of A.D. Cornets de Groot (assistant residency official in Surakarta) to his parents in the Netherlands, dated Surakarta, 2 August 1825 (pp. 15–16). Returning urgently from Batavia overland via Semarang and Magelang in company with Resident of Kedhu De Clercq (25–27 July 1825), Cornets de Groot describes the initial shockwave of the Java War. He marvels at the extraordinary coolness and composure of Resident Hendrik Mauritz MacGillavry, which he views as crucial in maintaining the loyalty of the Surakarta Kraton and preventing Sunan Pakubuwana VI from joining Dipanagara. Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock arrives in Surakarta, establishing an aura of confidence and stability. Cornets de Groot reports that insurgent mobs—composed of rural vagabonds and tramps (*landlopers*) armed with cudgels, stones, and slings—have rallied to Dipanagara and Mangkubumi, slaughtering Chinese and Europeans and laying total siege to Yogyakarta, effectively severing communications and isolating colonial garrisons. In Kedhu, rebel bands attack Magelang six times, where the small garrison of 500 men successfully beats them back despite the lack of a proper fortification. In contrast, Surakarta remains peaceful, fortified by joint patrols of the Mangkunagaran Legion and Sunan's troops. Cornets de Groot expresses deep admiration for De Kock's calm prudence, predicting that the General will resolve the crisis more effectively through astute political statecraft than through brute military force.

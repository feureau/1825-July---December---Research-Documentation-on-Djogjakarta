## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Textile Trade & Porterage Economics (13 Jan 1825):** Detailed commercial record showing Yogyakarta's textile industry producing batiks/sarongs for export via the port of Semarang. Wages for overland coolies were precisely calculated at 3 *duits* (copper doits) per league (*paal* / league), reflecting colonial labor cost controls.
- **Toll Deficits (17 Jan 1825):** Defaulting Chinese tax farmers at the crucial eastern mountain pass gate of **Gunung Tawang** (Madiun border) indicate broader economic distress and cash shortages in the Principalities before the outbreak of war.
- **Agricultural Production in Pacitan (17 Jan 1825):** 1,004 *piculs* of coffee (approx. 62 metric tons) and 144 *piculs* of pepper shipped to the colonial treasury in Batavia.
- **Residency Personnel & J.F. Dietrée (19 Jan 1825):** J.F. Dietrée, a fixture of the Yogyakarta Residency since W.H. van IJsseldijk's residency in 1796, petitioned for retirement. Despite over 28 years of service, he earned less than newly arrived Dutch language cadets (*élèves voor de Javaansche taal*), suffering deafness, physical deformity, and poverty. Smissaert recommended a monthly pension of 75 guilders.
- **Tissot's Petition (19 Jan 1825):** Refers to a French merchant/resident in Yogyakarta facing ruin following economic contraction and the revocation of land rentals.

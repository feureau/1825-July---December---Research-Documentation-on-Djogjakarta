## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0144.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Collectie De Kock No. 183 - Surakarta Residency Letters / Report of Inspector Wörmer from Pacitan, Part II)
- **Date Range:** `1825-08-11` to `1825-08-11`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Concluding section of the eyewitness report by Wörmer (*Leerling in de Javaansche Taal* / *Opziener* of Pacitan) detailing the fall of Pacitan on 11 August 1825. In his circular dispatch, Pangéran Dipanagara explicitly frames the insurrection as a religious holy war (*prang sabil*), summoning the Javanese population to revolt for the sake of the Muslim religion against the Dutch and all overseas foreign nations (*wong sabrang*) to expel all foreigners from Java. The government-appointed Regent, Radèn Tumenggung Djasa Karia (Bupati Djimat), who is illiterate in Javanese script, orders his wife to compose an affirmative reply submitting to Dipanagara. Demang Dipa Mengala of Desa Madja arrives as an emissary from Ahmad Daris, ordering the immediate arrest and seizure of Pacitan's Chinese opium tax-farmer. Facing imminent execution, Inspector Wörmer escapes from the regency disguised as an Islamic pilgrim (*hajji*). Meanwhile, Ahmad Daris marches into Pacitan at the head of merely 60 armed insurgents, meeting no resistance and rapidly winning over the local *demangs*. Wörmer observes a crucial socio-political reality of rural Java: the village populations (*desas*) invariably follow the political stance of their *demangs*, allowing Ahmad Daris to capture the entire regency effortlessly. Wörmer's desperate attempts to rally resistance are actively sabotaged by Karia Redja (Head Bekel of Karang Weda and son of Djasa Karia). Wörmer concludes with the grim political maxim: *"once regent goes over, ENTIRE REGION falls"*.

  - **Key Entities (Places):** Pacitan (Patjitan), Desa Madja, Karang Weda, Java

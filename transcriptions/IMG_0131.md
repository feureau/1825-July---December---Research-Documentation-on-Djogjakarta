## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0131.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Research Notes (Typewritten transcript of Radèn Mas Suwangsa's report to Lieutenant-General De Kock in ARA Collectie De Kock / H.F. Aukes, *Het Legioen van Mangkoe-Negoro*, Bandung 1935, pp. 79–81)
- **Date Range:** 1825-07-28 to 1825-07-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Firsthand intelligence debriefing of Radèn Mas Suwangsa, cavalry captain of the Mangkunagaran Legion, reporting to Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock regarding his combat capture and detention at Pangéran Dipanagara's headquarters at Selarong between 28 and 31 July 1825 (ARA Collectie De Kock; Aukes 1935, pp. 79–81). On 28 July 1825, Suwangsa leads a relief detachment comprising 25 infantrymen and 12 dragoons attempting to breach the rebel siege encircling Yogyakarta. The detachment is surrounded and cut to pieces outside the city; fighting fiercely, Suwangsa reaches Klaten accompanied by only two surviving dragoons, but is struck unconscious on the back of the head by a slingstone (*bandring*) and taken prisoner. Brought before Dipanagara at Selarong on 29 July, Suwangsa steadfastly refuses to defect or accept a rebel commission under a white ceremonial parasol (*pajung*), proclaiming he would rather die than betray his father-in-law, Pangéran Adipati Arya Mangkunagara II; Dipanagara releases him with an armed peasant escort to Delanggu. Suwangsa's report provides an extraordinary eyewitness survey of Selarong: Dipanagara is dressed in white priestly robes, attended by Pangéran Mangkubumi, Pangéran Adinegara (serving as army commander), and Princes Adiwinata, Suryadipura, and Blitar (with Pangéran Adisurya operating in Kedu and Dipakusuma at Pegunungan Pager). The princes and bupatis lodge in the local *demang's* house with only 150–200 retainers; no static fortifications or trenches exist, though the access road from Yogya is fortified with pitfalls and sharpened bamboo stakes (*ranjau*). Suwangsa notes that the princes sleep until 9 or 10 a.m. and consume opium, possessing roughly 30 rifles and numerous pikes. Interrogations reveal that the princes revolted primarily due to the intolerable contempt of Patih Danureja IV (who treated them as common subjects) and the disrespectful arrogance of Assistant Resident P.F.H. Chevallier; having taken the step, they are determined to fight to the end.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Radèn Mas Suwangsa, Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock, Pangéran Dipanagara (*DP*), Pangéran Adipati Arya Mangkunagara II (*MN II*), Pangéran Mangkubumi, Pangéran Adinegara, Pangéran Adiwinata, Pangéran Suryadipura, Pangéran Blitar, Pangéran Adisurya, Radèn Mas Dipa Atmaja / Dipakusuma, Radèn Adipati Danureja IV (*Danuredja*), P.F.H. Chevallier, H.F. Aukes.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta (*Jogja*), Klaten (*Klatten*), Selarong, Delanggu, Kedu, Pegunungan Pager (*Pager Mt*).
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Mangkunagaran Legion Cavalry Detachment, Siege of Yogyakarta, Slingstone Wound (*bandring*), Eyewitness Interrogation at Selarong, White Parasol (*white pajung*), Rebel Leadership Structure, Pitfalls & Bamboo Stakes (*ranjau*), Opium Consumption, Aristocratic Grievances against Danureja IV and Chevallier.

In Selarong itself no patrols were kept and no look-outs posted.

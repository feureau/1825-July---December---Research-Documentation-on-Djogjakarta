## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Market Tolls & Fiscal Pressure (24 Jan 1825):** Chinese tax farmer San Hiang Sing begged to be relieved of tax contracts for the *begalan* (market barrier tolls) of **Bantul** and **Jatinom**. The astronomical lease prices demanded by the colonial state (over 41,000 guilders for Bantul alone in 1824) triggered violent local resentment and resistance, directly preconditioning the rural peasantry around Bantul and Selarong to join Diponegoro in July 1825.
- **Ex-Sultan Hamengkubuwana II in Exile (27 Jan 1825):** Sultan Sepuh, exiled by Raffles to Penang in 1812 and later relocated by the Dutch to Ambon and then Surabaya, lived in isolation with his primary consort **Ratu Kencana Wulan** (originally a betel/sirih vendor who became politically dominant during his second reign). Despite colonial clearance for loyalists to visit, his harsh previous rule meant few sent gifts or maintained contact. He would later be restored by the Dutch in August 1826 as a counterweight to Diponegoro.
- **Cholera Morbus Epidemic (4 Feb 1825):** The global cholera pandemic hit Yogyakarta with catastrophic severity in early 1825. The high mortality, burning of corpses, and inadequate medical response deepened popular belief that the colonial regime had brought cosmic imbalance (*kualat*) and divine retribution to Java.
- **Medical Administration:** Smissaert notes the assistance of Dr. Vogeler. Meanwhile, smallpox vaccination across the Vorstenlanden remained chaotic, prompting Surakarta Resident Van Sevenhoven to travel personally to Batavia.

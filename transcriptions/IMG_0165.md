# Transcription of IMG_0165.jpg

## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0165.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (*Diary of Van der Capellen, July 1825 – Jan 1826*; *Tijdschrift voor Neêrland's Indië* [TNI], 1860, vol. 11, p. 367).
- **Date Range:** 1825-07-26 to 1825-08-05
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Journal entries of Governor-General Van der Capellen documenting the strategic military crisis facing the Netherlands Indies government in late July and early August 1825. Van der Capellen records with deep alarm the extreme depletion of Dutch military forces across Java, resulting from heavy troop commitments to external expeditions in West Borneo (Pontianak) and South Celebes (Bone/Makassar expedition under General Van Geen). Out of 700 men stationed at Weltevreden (Batavia), only 150 can be spared and dispatched in haste by sea to Semarang to support Lieutenant-Governor-General De Kock. Van der Capellen warns that Dutch garrisons across Java have never been so dangerously undermanned, creating an acute risk that the secretly orchestrated rebellion will spark a general island-wide conflagration, exacerbated by the formidable moral influence of the Princely Courts and Islamic religious leaders (*"fanaticism of priests"*). Dispatches from Yogyakarta are largely severed, with only a few reports slipping through via trusted native couriers, revealing widespread pillaging around Yogya and Fort Klaten. In Kedu, Lieutenant-Colonel Frans David Cochius operates to relieve besieged posts, but wildly exaggerated rumors circulate that 1,800 to 6,000 men have been slaughtered near Magelang. Although Captain Duperron fights his way through to Magelang to succor the beleaguered European community, the defenders are driven close to despair by fears that overland supply routes from Semarang are permanently cut, stoking dread of the imminent collapse of European rule.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Hendrik Merkus de Kock, Lieutenant-Colonel Frans David Cochius, Captain Duperron, General Josephus Jacobus van Geen, Pangéran Dipanagara.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Semarang, Batavia (Weltevreden), Borneo (Kalimantan), Makassar (Celebes/Sulawesi), Yogyakarta, Klaten (Fort Klaten), Magelang, Kedu, Java.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Military Garrison Depletion, External Expeditions (Bone / Borneo), Island-Wide Insurrection Risk, Court Prestige, Religious Mobilization (*"fanaticism of priests"* / santri), Courier Communication Lines, Rumor Inflation / Panic, Overland Supply Lines.

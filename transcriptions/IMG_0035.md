## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **The Fourfold Indigenous Judiciary of Pre-War Yogyakarta:** This document provides an exceptional structural anatomy of the four specialized judicial organs in the Sultanate of Yogyakarta:
  1. **Pengadilan Surambi (The Islamic Sharia Court):** Convened on the open verandah (*surambi*) of the Great Mosque, headed by Mas Pengulu. Decided family law, successions, oaths, and capital crimes where Islamic jurisprudence applied. Its rulings were reviewed by the Patih and the royal council of *Nayaka* before Sultanic endorsement.
  2. **Pengadilan Pradata (The Royal Civil and Criminal Court):** Headed by Mas Ngabèhi Nitipraja and 24 *mantri jějining* and *kori* (court heralds and assessors). Handled serious felonies, arson, cattle rustling, and instances of violent frenzy (*amuk tariak*).
  3. **Mancanagara Regional Jurisdictions:** Eastern Mancanagara bupatis adjudicated local theft, property, and civil disputes in their outer provinces. However, capital homicides and contested verdicts could be appealed directly to Yogyakarta, where visiting bupatis were frequently overruled by the central court. Regional pengulus handled marital law with a right of appeal to the Mas Pengulu in Yogyakarta.
  4. **Pengadilan Balé Mangu (The Inter-Ethnic / Administrative Court):** Seated in the Grand Vizier's compound (*Kepatihan*) under Major Wiranagara, Tumenggung Sindunagara, and the judicial prosecutors (*jaksa*). It held exclusive competence over jurisdictional boundary disputes between royal appanages, bureaucratic infractions by mantris, and all civil litigation, debts, and commercial transactions involving Javanese and Europeans.

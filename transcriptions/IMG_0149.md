## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0149.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Collectie De Kock No. 183 - Solo Letters 1825–1826 / Rebellion in Eastern Mancanagara & Slopes of Mount Lawu)
- **Date Range:** `1825-09-15` to `1826-01-04`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Epistolary extracts and military intelligence dispatches documenting court diplomacy in Surakarta and the counter-insurgency campaign along the slopes of Mount Lawu. On 15 September 1825 (confirmed 13 October / received Batavia 17 December 1825), Governor-General Van der Capellen ratifies major royal promotions to solidify aristocratic loyalty: Pangéran Aria Buminata is elevated to the supreme rank of Panembahan, while Pangéran Aria Purbaya (the future Pakubuwana VII) and Pangéran Aria Ngabèhi are created Pangéran Adipati. On 24 December 1825, a combat report from Desa Simok in Madiun (in the Lawu foothills) describes an engagement fought by royal auxiliary troops from Pamekasan (Madura) and Kediri against entrenched insurgents. Positioned on high ground with firearms, the rebels kill the second son of the Prince of Pamekasan, triggering confusion among Madurese ranks. However, the well-disciplined Kediri force, commanded by Major Brun and commended by Resident of Rembang P.H. van Lawick van Pabst, successfully rallies and repels the rebel host, inflicting 20–25 fatalities against 2–3 government casualties, amidst torrential monsoon rains. On 4 January 1826, a dispatch from Jagaraga reports that Major Elout and reserve columns disperse the insurgents, driving rebel commanders Pangéran Serang and Radèn Adipati Kertadirja to flee into the upper recesses of Mount Lawu.

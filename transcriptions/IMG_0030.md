## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **The 31 January 1824 Ultimatum:** Following Governor-General Van der Capellen's landmark resolution of 6 May 1823 abolishing land leases in the Principalities, foreign leaseholders were given a grace period ending on 31 January 1824 to surrender their estates. Those remaining in possession without explicit government exemption were denied residency rights and subject to deportation.
- **Impact on the Chinese Community:** While European planters were partly indemnified and frequently remained on the land as government estate managers (e.g. Boutet, Dezentjé), Chinese leaseholders and sub-renters faced severe discrimination, sudden loss of capital, and forced expulsion from the rural countryside (*désas*) into designated urban ghettos (*pecinan*). This exacerbated economic distress, severed rural credit networks, and deepened ethnic and social fractures on the eve of the Java War.

- **Date Range:** `1825-07-28` to `1825-08-09`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Carey's original handwritten archival research notes taken directly from the Dutch official dossier in Collectie De Kock No. 183, containing the intelligence debriefing of Radèn Mas Suwangsa (an officer in the Mangkunagaran Legion and son-in-law of Pangéran Prangwedana / Mangkunagara II). On 28 July 1825, Suwangsa led a relief detachment of 12 dragoons and 21 infantry carrying vital dispatches from Surakarta to Yogyakarta. Near Demangan (four leagues east of Yogya), the column was surrounded and fiercely attacked by thousands of insurgent peasants and village heads. Running out of ammunition at Prambanan, Suwangsa was felled from his horse by sling-stones (*bandring*). Before he could be speared to death, two surviving dragoons intervened, shouting *"Gusti-ku!"* and appealing to Demang Setra Redja of Kalasan that Suwangsa was the son-in-law of Mangkunagara II. Spared and conveyed under guard via Plered to Dipanagara's military camp at Selarong, Suwangsa was interrogated by Dipanagara, who offered him a white sunshade (*payung*) and top military command if he defected. Suwangsa steadfastly refused, citing his sacred oath to his prince and father-in-law, and was eventually escorted safely to Delanggu on 31 July. Suwangsa's report provides an invaluable firsthand profile of the rebel headquarters: Dipanagara robed in white priestly attire, princes lodged informally in the local Demang's residence, extensive opium smoking, a complete lack of military redoubts or entrenchments, and fierce grievances centered on hatred for Patih Danureja IV and Assistant Resident Chevallier.

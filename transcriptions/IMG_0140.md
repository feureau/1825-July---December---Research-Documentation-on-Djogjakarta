# Transcription of IMG_0140.jpg

## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0140.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (A.D. Cornets de Groot Papers, pt. 6, Koninklijke Bibliotheek, The Hague)
- **Date Range:** `1825-08-23` to `1825-09-20`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Private epistolary accounts of A.D. Cornets de Groot (assistant residency official in Surakarta) to his brother J.P. de Groot and parents, chronicling military developments across Java between late August and late September 1825. Cornets de Groot describes a relentless administrative workload as Dipanagara's insurgent forces expand daily, coercing and burning non-compliant villages. By 30 August, rebel bands have severed the Great Post Road (*De Grote Postweg*) near Demak. By mid-September, Major-General Josephus Jacobus van Geen returns from the expedition to Bone/Macassar to Surabaya, arriving in Surakarta and launching counter-offensives against the forces of the prominent female commander Radèn Ayu Serang at Demak. Colonial columns begin regaining ground across Solo, Yogya, Kedhu, Banyumas, and Klaten, bolstered by 500 European veterans from Macassar and 1,200 Madurese auxiliaries from Semarang. Meanwhile, Dipanagara concentrates his main host at Selarong to defend against an impending grand assault by Lieutenant-General De Kock. By 20 September, De Kock and Resident MacGillavry prepare to march in force from Surakarta on Selarong to crush the rebellion, leaving Cornets de Groot in sole administrative charge of Surakarta. The letters reveal acute panic in Batavia, where 14 ships are mobilized to convoy troops to Semarang while fearful European civilians openly contemplate evacuating Java entirely and returning to Europe.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** A.D. Cornets de Groot, Pangéran Dipanagara, Major-General Josephus Jacobus van Geen, Radèn Ayu Serang, Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock, Hendrik Mauritz MacGillavry, J.P. de Groot
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Surakarta (Solo / Sala), Demak, Kedhu, Surabaya, Makassar (Macassar), Banyumas, Klaten, Selarong, Yogyakarta, Semarang, Batavia

| **[30 Aug 1825]**<br>*(p. 19)*<br>*(CdeG to Parents)* | **Disruption of Northern Communications:**<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Post Road Severed:* The Great Post Road (*postweg*) leading to Demak has been severed by rebel detachments.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Personal Health:* Notes that Cornets de Groot is suffering from chronic liver illness. |
| **[13 Sept 1825]**<br>*(pp. 20–21)*<br>*(CdeG to J.P. de Groot)* | **Arrival of Macassar Veterans & Northern Counter-Offensive:**<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Van Geen's Return:* Major-General Josephus Jacobus van Geen returns from the expedition to Macassar [South Sulawesi] to Surabaya.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Battle at Demak:* Arriving at Solo, Van Geen coordinates attacks against the forces of Radèn Ayu Serang at Demak.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Regaining Territory:* Colonial forces begin regaining lost ground across Solo, Yogya, Kedhu, Banyumas, and Klaten.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Selarong Stronghold:* Dipanagara's main army is concentrated at Selarong to defend the mountain stronghold, awaiting an assault by General De Kock.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Reinforcements:* 500 European troops arrive from Macassar; awaiting 1,200 Madurese auxiliary troops from Semarang. |
| **[20 Sept 1825]**<br>*(pp. 22–23)*<br>*(CdeG to J.P. de Groot)* | **Impending General Assault on Selarong & Batavia Panic:**<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Peaceful Solo:* Surakarta remains very peaceful while Dipanagara masses his forces at Selarong.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Assault Planned in 2–3 Days:* De Kock to march from Solo on Selarong within 2–3 days; colonial command hopes the capture of Selarong will bring an immediate end to the war.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Madurese Successes:* Dipanagara's forces suffer successive tactical defeats at the hands of Dutch and Madurese troops.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Cornets de Groot in Command:* Resident MacGillavry marches alongside De Kock to Yogya and Selarong; Cornets de Groot is left in charge of the Surakarta residency administration; with most of the garrison deployed to Yogya, Solo is very quiet.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• *Batavia Evacuation Panic:* 14 transport ships sail from Batavia to Semarang; the European civilian population in Batavia is gripped by panic and actively contemplating evacuating Java entirely to sail back to Europe. |

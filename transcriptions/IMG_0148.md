- **Date Range:** `1825-09-30` to `1825-11-25`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Archival research notes from Collectie De Kock No. 183 synthesizing dispatches from A.D. Cornets de Groot and Resident Hendrik Mauritz MacGillavry to Governor-General Van der Capellen, tracing military developments and shifting alliances across the Eastern Mancanagara between late September and late November 1825. On 30 September 1825, loyal Surakarta bupatis in the eastern districts—led by Pangéran Aria Cakradiningrat of Kediri alongside the bupatis of Andhong and Jarengan—consolidate forces to repel Yogyakarta insurgent columns. Three loyal Surakarta bupatis in Panaraga (Ponorogo) deploy to sever communications and prevent a merger between the eastern rebel regencies and the main theater of war in Mataram. In the south, Pacitan and Sembuyang remain engulfed in rebellion. P.H. van Lawick van Pabst advises Surakarta authorities that the two senior Yogyakarta *bupati wedanas* of Madiun—Radèn Mas Tumenggung Prawiradiningrat and Radèn Tumenggung Rangga Prawirasentika—have written secretly to the Patih of Solo declaring that their outward compliance with Dipanagara was extracted under extreme duress by the *brandals*. By 4 October 1825, peace is restored across the core Surakarta residency, though rebel bands probe the district of Bala attempting to establish contact with Pangéran Serang. By 25 November 1825, Surakarta is completely secure; Pangéran Aria Kusumayuda and Inner Regent Judanegara lead royal columns to Kedhu to fight insurgents there. The Eastern Mancanagara pacification advances rapidly, with only three regents remaining in rebellion, culminating in the total defeat of the rebels at Katongga, which shatters Radèn Adipati Kertadirja's planned offensive against Ketangga.

## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Colonial Encroachment on Princely Enclaves (Jabarangkah):** The Jabarangkah territories were scattered enclaves in the central mountain spine (Dieng, Karangkobar, Batur, Bagelen) owned by the courts of Surakarta and Yogyakarta but completely surrounded by Government territory. By the Council resolution of 22 February 1825 No. 7, Governor-General Van der Capellen moved to annex these lands unilaterally to rationalize provincial tax boundaries, placing the inhabitants under direct colonial corvée to service the Great Post Road (*Grote Postweg*) and coffee cultivation.
- **Compensation Formula:** The Dutch proposed paying annual tax indemnities to the courts in exchange for direct sovereign jurisdiction, an arrangement that stripped the royal princes of traditional feudal authority and heightened court grievances.
- **Precedents on Land and Chinese Pacht (1822–1823):** Carey notes the strict exclusionary policies preceding the Java War: the 8 February 1822 resolution disciplining the Chinese tollgate keeper of Sragen for illegally acquiring rural land, and the sweeping *Besluit* of 6 May 1823 prohibiting all non-Javanese from leasing estate lands or villages across Yogyakarta, Surakarta, and the Mangkunagaran.

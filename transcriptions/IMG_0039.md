## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Majapahit Heritage & Royal Legitimacy:** In the introductory cantos of the *Babad Dipanagara*, Diponegoro traces his spiritual lineage from Raden Bondan Kejawan (Ki Ageng Tarub III), son of the last Majapahit monarch King Brawijaya V. Colonial historian P.J.F. Louw dismissed this crucial royal genealogy as irrelevant, exemplifying the Eurocentric blind spots of early colonial historiography.
- **Smissaert's Fatal Misreading of Diponegoro:** Drewes exposes Resident A.H. Smissaert's dispatch to Governor-General Van der Capellen penned on 19 July 1825—the very eve of the clash at Tegalrejo. Incapable of comprehending the profound Javanese-Islamic religious devotion and prophetic vocation animating Diponegoro, Smissaert diagnosed his retreat and ascetic exercises (*tapa*) as bouts of temporary insanity (*zinneloosheid*) and religious fanaticism. Drewes brands this report an embarrassing *testimonium paupertatis* (certificate of intellectual bankruptcy) for a colonial official who had served 24 years in Java.
- **Du Bus de Gisignies' Structural Assessment:** In contrast to Smissaert's superficial disparagement, Commissioner-General Viscount L.P.J. du Bus de Gisignies later acknowledged in his confidential reports to the Dutch Minister of Colonies that the deep structural injustices, land confiscations, and fiscal abuses suffered by the Javanese made Diponegoro's uprising fully understandable.

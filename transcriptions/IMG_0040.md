## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **The Concept of Erucakra / Ratu Adil:** When Diponegoro proclaimed holy war (*Prang Sabil*) at Selarong in late July 1825, he assumed the regal-messianic title **Ingkang Sinuhun Kangjeng Sultan Ngabdulkamid Erucakra Kabirul Mukminin Sayidin Panatagama Kalifatullah Tanah Jawa**. Drewes emphasizes that adopting the name **Erucakra** anchored his movement directly in the millenarian *Ratu Adil* (Just King) prophecies ascribed to the 12th-century King Jayabaya of Kediri, merged with Islamic concepts of the *Khalifah* and *Mahdi*.
- **Peter Carey's Historiographical Focus:** Carey's red marginal notation (*"Importance of DP as Erucatra"*) captures the central interpretive breakthrough of his own lifetime scholarship: that Diponegoro cannot be understood simply as a disgruntled feudal prince reacting to tax policies, nor as a delusional fanatic, but as a charismatic religious leader operating within a coherent Javanese-Islamic cosmological framework.
- **Flaws of Western Psychological Analogies:** Drewes systematically refutes Dutch scholar P.H. van der Kemp's famous literary thesis casting Diponegoro as a Javanese "Hamlet" paralyzed by indecision at the decadent court of Yogyakarta. Such European models ignored the profound indigenous reality of Javanese Islamic mysticism (*kejawen*), ascetic spiritual preparation (*tirakat*), and prophetic mission.

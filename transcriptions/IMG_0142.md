- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (A.D. Cornets de Groot Papers, pt. 6 / Bataviasche Courant, Koninklijke Bibliotheek, The Hague)
- **Date Range:** `1825-10-05` to `1825-11-25`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Epistolary extracts and published press dispatches detailing the two colonial stormings of Dipanagara's headquarters at Selarong and military actions across Central and East Java in October and November 1825. On 5 October 1825, Cornets de Groot reports the initial capture of Selarong; rebel princes flee into the hills but quickly reoccupy the position once colonial forces withdraw, prompting a second punitive expedition. The temporary clearing of Selarong brings visible relief to the countryside: peasants return to cultivate their villages (*desas*), traders re-enter Yogyakarta, and acute food shortages in the Sultan's capital subside. On 7 October, Selarong is stormed and conquered a second time; Dipanagara narrowly escapes on horseback after his mount becomes mired in a muddy morass, evading capture solely because pursuing Dutch cavalry horses are completely exhausted. The rebels suffer 50 dead. According to the *Bataviasche Courant* (19 October 1825), General J.J. van Geen returns on 11 October from pursuing Dipanagara along the Progo River, receiving the surrender of the Demang of Gamping, while Captain Kilian at Karanggèdhé (Gagatan) repulses 2,000 insurgents led by Radèn Sukur (Radèn Hasan Mahmud). By 25 November 1825, Surakarta is tranquil; Pangéran Kusumayuda departs on a roving royal commission to Bagelen to exploit his influential family ties in Kedhu on behalf of the Sunan. In the eastern *mancanagara*, royalist Surakarta *bupatis* and Captain Theunissen attack rebel positions at Jagaraga, killing 50–60 insurgents; Carey highlights the actions of the "famous" Mas Tumenggung Kertadirja, who torches Magetan and marches with 600 men against Jagaraga before being checked by the Panaraga regents.

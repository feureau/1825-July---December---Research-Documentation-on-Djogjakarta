## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Land Restitution Difficulties (3 Jan 1825):** The decree of 6 May 1823 required return of leased lands to indigenous landholders, but indigenous officials (such as *Mas Behi Jasa Wedana*) struggled to reimburse the required indemnities to Chinese and European leaseholders (e.g. Tan Tan Lok). Consequently, Jasa Wedana re-leased peripheral *arimbon* lands in Bagelen to another Chinese lessee, Limo Han, demonstrating widespread evasion of the 1823 abolition decree.
- **Toll-Gate Abuses (4 Jan 1825):** Toll-gates (*bandar*) leased by Chinese tax-farmers (e.g. Tan Buntjut) caused rampant friction across inter-residensi borders (Kediri, Kertosono, Surakarta, and Yogyakarta). Complaints from regional regents (*Bupati Tjakradiningrat*) led to formal extradition and trial in Surakarta.
- **Pacitan Infrastructure (6 Jan 1825):** The new overland road from Surakarta to Pacitan was a strategic colonial project initiated to bypass hazardous coastal sea transport and improve the extraction of cash crops (pepper, coffee).
- **Residency Personnel (8 Jan 1825):** Resident A.H. Smissaert advocates for the pay scales of long-serving colonial functionaries: J.F. Dietzten (veteran since the Daendels/British administrations) and W.V. de Meijer (former Dutch cavalry cadet who joined Baron Nahuys van Burgst's retinue in Yogyakarta).

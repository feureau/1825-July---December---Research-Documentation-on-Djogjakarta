## Metadata

- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Algemeen Rijksarchief / ANRI *Yogya Letters* No. 22, 1825–1826).
- **Date Range:** 1824-12-28 to 1825-05-20
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** The report reviews the administrative and financial complications surrounding the government-leased frontier districts of Jabarangkah and Karangkobar. Although poorly suited for coffee cultivation, these enclave territories held extreme strategic value, linking commercial routes to Kedu and commanding key interior transit corridors hampered by royal tollgates (*bandar*). The Vorstenlanden Commission recommended against retroceding these lands to their original court owners due to their vast geographical distance from Surakarta and Yogyakarta, advising instead that the colonial government take them over permanently on long-term lease. By Governor-General Decree No. 13 of 28 December 1824, the Commission was authorized to conclude formal treaties with the Sunan and Sultan for the definitive leasing/surrender of Jabarangkah and Karangkobar to the government. On 20 May 1825, the Commission submitted a follow-up report highlighting a severe economic collapse in the coffee sector: a catastrophic drop in coffee market prices triggered by the abrupt abandonment and neglect of plantations, combined with the protracted timeline required to repay heavy indemnity claims (*restituties*) to dispossessed European planters.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Governor-General G.A.G.Ph. van der Capellen, Sunan of Surakarta, Sultan of Yogyakarta, Vorstenlanden Commission Members (Van Sevenhoven, Smissaert, MacGillavry).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Jabarangkah, Karangkobar, Surakarta (Solo), Yogyakarta, Kedu, Pekalongan, Semarang.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Frontier enclaves, *landverhuur* (rural land leases), Governor-General resolution, tollgate abolition, coffee price collapse, planter indemnity repayments (*restituties*).

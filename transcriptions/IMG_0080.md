- **Date Range:** 1825-07-20 to 1825-07-23
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Carey reconstructs the critical operational chronology of the outbreak of the Java War between 20 and 23 July 1825. On the morning of 20 July, Pangéran Mangkubumi is sent to Tegalrejo amid panic in Yogyakarta, where markets close, villagers along main highways flee or hide property, and court leaders—Ratu Ageng, Paku Alam I, and Patih Danureja IV—press for decisive military action. Following a failed final diplomatic overture via Sindunegara and Mentura, an expeditionary strike force is formed comprising colonial infantry and cavalry, Kraton prajurits under Major Tumenggung Wiranagara, and Pakualaman dragoons led by Suryaningrat, Suryaningprang, and Suryaputra. The assault on Dipanagara's dalem results in fierce resistance: Sergeant David and two dragoons are killed, trumpet signals fail, and after suffering 15–20 casualties, Dipanagara and Mangkubumi break through colonial lines toward Kali Noka near Bantul Karang where Adinegara awaits. Following a further Dutch attack at Kali Noka on 21 July, the princes withdraw to Blimbing on the strategic road to Pacitan. On 22 July, Mangkunagaran Legion troops arrive under Colonel von Jett and Captain Keur. On the morning of 23 July 1825, Captain Keur's 120-man detachment is ambushed and shattered at Pisangan (documented in Schoemaker's *"Gedong Inggris"*, TNI 1902), with two chests of silver currency, 40 muskets, and extensive ammunition seized by rebels. Simultaneously, the Surakarta Patih alerts border posts at Sukawati, Grojogan, and Babadan, coffee laborers flee plantations at Kasihan and Rejawinangun, and general rural revolt ignites with the burning and complete destruction of colonial tollgates (*bandar*) at Narawalong, Cemen, Kemlaka, and Gamping.

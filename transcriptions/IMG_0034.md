## Contextual Notes & Analysis
- **Erosion of Princely Judicial Sovereignty (1812–1825):** Following Stamford Raffles' storming of the Yogyakarta Kraton in June 1812, the Dutch-British colonial regimes progressively curtailed royal criminal and civil jurisdiction. MacGillavry's dispatch of 29 June 1825 (written mere weeks before the outbreak of the Java War) outlines proposals to strip the rulers of jurisdiction over all non-indigenous populations (Chinese, Arabs, Indian "Moors") and inter-jurisdictional disputes, relegating princely authority exclusively to ethnic Javanese subjects.
- **The Surambi Islamic Court Structure:** The document preserves Major Radèn Tumenggung Wiranagara's vital internal survey of Yogyakarta's Islamic court (*Pengadilan Surambi*), which sat on the open verandah (*surambi*) of the Grand Mosque (*Mesjid Gedhe Kauman*). Presided over by the royal chief cleric (*Mas Pengulu*) and 36 subordinate officials (court preachers/*kětib*, theologians/*ulama*, summoners/*kabayan*, and mosque caretakers/*mirbot*), the Surambi adjudicated Islamic family law, estates, contracts, oaths, and capital offenses (murder/retaliation) alongside customary law.

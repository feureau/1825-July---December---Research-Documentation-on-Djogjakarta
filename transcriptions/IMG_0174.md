# Transcription of IMG_0174.jpg

## Metadata
- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0174.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes on the Java War (1825–1830) and Yogyakarta History (Extracts from Governor-General G.A.G.Ph. Baron van der Capellen's Private Diary, *Tijdschrift voor Neêrland's Indië*, 1860, vol. 11, pp. 360–387)
- **Date Range:** `1825-08-25 to 1825-09-27`
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Rapid expansion of the northern insurgency in late August and September 1825; rebel forces overwhelm Land Tax Inspector Nagel's detachment, capture two colonial field artillery pieces, and inflict devastating casualties on Resident Pinket van Haak's retreating troops; widespread panic erupts over imminent attacks against the residencies of Kudus and Pati; Surakarta subjects in Grobogan rise in defiance, threatening to sever the coastal trunk line between Semarang and Rembang; Major-General Joseph van Geen returns from the expedition to South Sulawesi (Makassar/Bone) and lands at Semarang with 350 European troops and Madurese auxiliaries from Sumenep; 700 regular troops are dispatched from Batavia to reinforce Cirebon's southern border; on 3 September 1825, Lieutenant-General De Kock sends formal peace letters to Dipanagara and Mangkubumi but receives no reply; Dipanagara adamantly rejects conferences and negotiations, citing the intolerable misconduct of Patih Danureja IV, Resident Smissaert, and Assistant Resident Chevallier; Sunan Pakubuwana VI also initiates correspondence, but Dipanagara replies that both the Sunan and Mangkunagara II are considered enemies for siding with the Dutch; ex-Sultan Hamengkubuwana II (Sultan Sepuh) is transferred under naval escort from Surabaya to Batavia due to fears of his clandestine involvement; Van der Capellen desires to deport the old Sultan to Bangka, but the Council of the Indies argues forcefully for restoring him to the Yogyakarta throne; Council member H.W. Muntinghe is commissioned to investigate restoration terms and draft letters from Sultan Sepuh to the insurgent princes; news of the capture of the Sultan of Palembang bolsters Dutch morale; by 27 September, conditions in Yogyakarta and Kedu remain desperate as Pangéran Sèrang's mobile bands terrorize Eastern Semarang.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Baron van der Capellen [Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies], Major-General Joseph van Geen [colonial commander returned from Makassar], Lieutenant-General Hendrik Merkus de Kock, Pangéran Dipanagara, Pangéran Mangkubumi, Sunan Pakubuwana VI, Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II), Sultan Hamengkubuwana II (Sultan Sepuh), Herman Warner Muntinghe [Member of the Council of the Indies], Diederik Willem Pinket van Haak [Resident of Japara], Inspector Nagel, Pangéran Sèrang.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Kudus, Pati, Grobogan, Semarang, Rembang, Makassar (Celebes), Sumenep (Madura), Cirebon (Cheribon), Surabaya, Batavia (Jakarta / Stadhuis), Bangka, Palembang, Yogyakarta (Djokja), Kedu.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Captured Artillery, Madurese Auxiliaries (*Barisan*), Council of the Indies (*Raad van Indië*), Restoration Policy, Surat Perjanjian / Peace Letters.
